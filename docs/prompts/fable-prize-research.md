## Work through evidence

Before each cycle, record one hypothesis, the baseline revision, input hashes,
evaluator revision, complete cost definition, negative controls and the result
that would defeat the hypothesis. Freeze those choices before looking at the
candidate's grade. Keep every attempted variant, including expansion and failure.

Develop the candidate and its exact inverse. A shared rule, reference, model or
dictionary is not free information: record where the decoder obtains it and how
it is charged or reconstructed. Evaluate restoration first, then bytes, time and
memory. Include the feature-disabled version and unrelated input. Preserve raw
commands, outputs, errors, dependency versions and machine details.

The current inspector checks supplied artifacts; its smoke runner executes a
fixed trusted codec. It is not an arbitrary entrant sandbox or a full Hutter
judge. Do not load candidate commands into it. Source-independent decoding needs
separate enforced input isolation. A fresh process does not establish that.

Request independent checking of frozen artifacts. You may diagnose a grader
defect, but may not improve the grade for your candidate by changing its checker
or expected inputs. Any evaluator correction gets separate review and a version,
then both the baseline and candidate run again. Retain the superseded evidence.

Use distinct examinations for public-prize performance, transfer to withheld
source groups, and generation of new outputs satisfying explicit properties.
Do not claim that exact recovery proves useful generation, or that a tuned
public-corpus result establishes general intelligence. Do not inspect reserved
evaluation answers through AUM history, logs or alternate paths.

receipt, every attempted variant, exact input/output hashes, all declared program
